# Know your loan terms

Loan to value
- Value of loan compared to value of home
It is the loan balance you owe, compared to the appraised value of the house.
If you apply for a loan program that requires a 95 percent LTV, it means you can borrow up to 95 percent of the home’s value.

Down Payment
- This is the amount of cash you pay toward the purchase price


Loan Term
- How much time to pay the loan. Usually 30 years
- The shorter the term, the higher the monthly payments, but the less interest you will pay over the life of the loan.

Interest rate

Annual Percentage Rate
To give you a true picture of the cost of the loan, lenders have to calculate an annual percentage Rate
The APR is the amount of interest you pay averaged over the life of the loan and includes the credit report, appraisal, loan processing and document preparation fees. When you are shopping for mortgage loan products, look at the APR to compare how much loans really cost

Total Interest Percentage
- This is total amount of interest you pay over the loan term as a percentage of your loan amount.

Points
- In addition to charging you interest each month, some lenders require you to pay points. 
- Each point equals one percent of your loan amount. 
- For example, if you were taking out a $95,000 loan, one point would cost $950. There are different kinds of points.

Discount Points
- Lower your interest rate, depending on how many points you pay.
- Each discount point usually lowers your interest rate by one-fourth of one percent
- So, if the current market rate is five percent, you could pay a lender one point to reduce it to 4.75 percent. If you want a lower rate and have some cash, you may be able to pay more discount points to lower the interest rate and save money over the long run

Lender Credits
- choose to have points paid to you and use them to cover some of your closing costs.


Origination Points
-  usually referred to as "points" are fees some lenders charge you just to make the loan. 
- Not all lenders charge an origination fee, and the amount of the fee varies among the lenders that do charge them.

Mortgage insurance
- if your down payment for a conventional or FHA loan is less than 20 percent of the home’s price. This insurance protects the lender in case you do not make your payments.

If you are getting a conventional loan, this kind of insurance is called Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI). If you are getting an FHA loan, it is called Mortgage Insurance Premium (MIP)  . With either one, you will usually pay a mortgage insurance fee at closing and then a monthly charge as part of your mortgage payment as well. The smaller the down payment, the higher your monthly premiums will be. Monthly mortgage insurance payments are included in lender’s housing ratios, and higher payments can decrease the amount you can afford to borrow.

Escrow Account
- As you learned before, your monthly house payment includes principal, interest, taxes and insurance payments. Property taxes, homeowners insurance and mortgage insurance are normally placed in an escrow account. The lender collects money each month as part of your monthly payment, saves it in the escrow account and then pays your tax and insurance bills for you when they are due.
Loan Fees
Fees  vary from one area to the next and from lender to lender. Ask your housing counselor and/or lender about average costs in your area. Before you agree to work with any lender, you should get a written estimate of all expenses it will charge, called a Loan Estimate. Ask when fees need to be paid. Usually fees for the application, credit report and appraisal are paid in advance at different times and are not refundable. If you think the fees are too much, check with other lenders to see if they charge the same amounts. Typical fees are listed below.
A loan application fee
- This fee is charged for taking your application and may or may not include the cost of the credit report and appraisal.
A credit report fee
-  covers the cost of a Mortgage Residential Credit Report.
An appraisal fee
- It pays for an appraisal report made by an appraiser to determine the market value of your home.
A loan origination fee
-  usually a percentage of the loan amount, covers the lender’s costs to prepare and review your loan. Some lenders charge this fee instead of separate fees for loan application, processing and underwriting.
A processing fee
-  covers the cost of putting together your loan package, including verification of your rent, employment and bank account balances.
An underwriting fee
- is charged to review and approve or deny the loan.
A document preparation fee
- is charged to prepare all of the documents needed for closing.

Prepayment
- Paying more than your regular payment is called partial prepayments. If you can afford to do it, making prepayments will help you to pay off your loan sooner and will save you a lot of money on interest.
Rate Lock
- s when a lender agrees to honor the current interest rate while your loan is being processed. Mortgage rates change often, and a rate lock allows you to finish the loan process knowing the exact interest rate you will be charged. This protects you if interest rates happen to go up, but also locks you in if rates go down. When you are shopping for a lender, ask when you can lock in a rate, how long it is good for and if the lender charges any rate lock in fees. Rate locks are typically available for 30, 45 or 60 days, and sometimes longer

Amortization
- paying back a loan a little at a time
When you first get your loan, most of your monthly payment will go toward interest. As you repay the amount you borrowed, your principal balance – the amount you still owe – goes down. Each month, a little more of your payment goes toward principal and a little less goes toward interest. Near the end of the loan, almost all of your payment is paying off your principal. Over time, your equity – the amount of your home that is paid for – goes up. Your lender can give you an amortization schedule that shows how much of each payment will go toward interest and how much will go toward principal throughout the length of the loan.
